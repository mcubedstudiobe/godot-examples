## How they were chosen

Each source page was opened and its licence read as Creative Commons 0. Every sound is a recording of a real object, never a synthesizer patch. Each was picked
from its page text and measured: duration, level, where its energy sits in the spectrum and how
fast it starts.

- **`rubber_chicken_squeak_a/b/c.wav`:** three different squeezes of real rubber chickens, picked so
  repeated hits do not sound identical: the lowest and broadest (fundamental about 450 Hz and a dull
  tone), a middle one (about 530 Hz) and a brighter one (about 700 Hz). Each starts in 10 to 20 ms
  and is cut to about half a second.
- **`rubber_chicken_crit.wav`:** two real rubber chicken recordings summed on their onsets, with
  the low end filtered out and the result gently clipped. It is about three to seven times brighter
  than the normal squeaks (86% of its energy above 4 kHz), starts in 5 ms, and is about 5 dB
  louder than the loudest of them.
- **`whoosh.wav`:** a soft, airy swish of a flexible hose through the air, chosen over stick and
  sword swings because its energy is spread from 500 Hz to 8 kHz instead of sitting in one band.
  It swells for about 0.13 s and fades out in about 0.2 s.
- **`soft_thump.wav`:** a hit on plush toys, dull with most of its energy under 500 Hz. It starts in
  15 ms and is back to silence within about 0.12 s, so it sits under the squeak without hiding it.
